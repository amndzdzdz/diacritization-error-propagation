## Position

If the measured degradation from automatic diacritization on Arabic MDD
turns out to be small, we report a small degradation, and the paper's
contribution rests on the decomposition protocol, the weighted-metric
result, and the bias-direction correction rather than on the magnitude of
the effect. We commit now to this framing regardless of what the numbers
say, specifically so that a disappointing effect size in December cannot
trigger a rewrite of the paper's argument under deadline pressure the way
it did in v1. Mathad (2021) and Kadambi (2024) already establish a prior
that upstream error's downstream impact on pronunciation scoring is minor
to moderate, not dominant, for a structurally similar problem; we will
quote that prior honestly rather than imply our effect must be larger
because our upstream component is diacritization rather than alignment.
We accept that a modest-effect paper is a harder sell at Interspeech than a
dramatic one, and that a modest effect plus a protocol and a miscalibrated-
metric result is a real paper, whereas a modest effect with nothing else
is not — which is why the protocol and the metric are written into the
plan as the load-bearing contributions (RQ2, RQ3) and the magnitude (RQ1)
is explicitly the cuttable, compact part.
